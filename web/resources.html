ColorMill Resources

This page collects the papers and specifications ColorMill v2 is built on, the working vocabulary, visual references and open questions we want available from the UI while designing the two-roll mill simulator.

Reference angle: the simulator looks at the mill from the front (+z toward the viewer): the bank on top of the two rolls, the nip between them, and the sheet wrapping the front roll. The camera orbits, but that is the home view.

Visual reference notes

  • The active mixing area is the narrow nip line/horizon where the two rollers press the material.
  • The top visible area is feed/entry into the nip, not a second mixer.
  • The bottom visible band is return/transport. It carries color state but should not continuously blur material like another nip.
  • The material should feel like a continuous sheet or curtain, not random fluid filling the entire screen.
  • Good visual behavior should produce elongated streaks, folding/banding behavior, and gradual homogenization over repeated passes.

Working vocabulary

TermDefinition
Two-roll millThe machine with two parallel rollers that pull, compress, sheet, and mix material.
RollerA rotating cylinder with diameter, width, RPM, surface speed, grip, and direction.
NipThe narrow gap where the rollers are closest. The primary active mixing event.
GapThe distance between rollers at the nip. Smaller gap means more compression and shear.
BankThe rolling slab of material resting on top of the two rolls, feeding the nip.
Sheet (band)The thin layer carried around the front roll after the nip; what you cut and fold.
Friction ratioBack roll surface speed divided by front roll surface speed. 1.0 is pure counter-rotation; above it the nip shears.
TackThe stickiness that makes the sheet follow a roll. In v2 the front roll is sticky (nodes near it take its velocity) and the back roll is a separating contact.
End guidesThe cheeks that confine the bank along the roll axis; the domain's x walls.
Shear rateHow fast neighbouring material slides past itself; read from the particle's affine velocity matrix and used to drive dispersion.
PassOne traversal of material through the nip.
Cut/foldManual operation that layers or reorients material before another pass.
DispersionHow evenly pigment has been broken up and distributed through material.
ConcentrationPigment mass divided by material mass in a cell or region.

Simulation concepts

Material point

A parcel of putty: position, velocity, APIC affine matrix, elastic deformation gradient and a Mixbox latent. 60k to ~1M of them depending on the preset.

Grid

The background lattice (h = 1/32 to 1/80) rebuilt every substep: particles scatter mass and momentum to it, it applies gravity and the boundaries, particles gather back.

Boundary

The rolls, walls and tray, applied to grid-node velocities: sticky (front roll), separating with Coulomb friction (back roll, floor), or free-slip (end walls).

Plastic return

After each step the singular values of the deformation gradient are clamped to a small yield band. This is what makes it putty and not a spring.

Dispersion

Once per frame each particle relaxes its latent toward its neighbourhood at a rate proportional to its shear rate: mixing only where the material is sheared.

Renderer

A ray-march through the packed density/latent volume: iso-surface, gradient normals, glossy shading, Mixbox decode per pixel; analytic rolls and tray.

Design questions

Answered in v2 (see project notes):

  1. Scale: a lab-size mill, abstracted — roller radius 0.32 sim units (about 64 mm at 0.2 m per unit), roll length 1.5, gap 0.02–0.10 exaggerated so the grid can resolve the sheet.
  2. Speed ratio: yes, as the continuous friction ratio (back / front, 1.0–1.6).
  3. Cut/fold: a scripted kinematic move of the visible sheet onto the bank, a quarter roll-length across.
  4. Mixing per pass: set by the dispersion rate acting on the shear rate in the nip; tune the single knob.

Still open:

  1. How should batch weight (bank size) be exposed — a seeding parameter, or added material at runtime?
  2. How should translucent silicone render differently from opaque white silicone (the ray-marcher is opaque today)?
  3. What is the simplest useful measure of dispersion quality to show in the HUD (latent variance in the sheet?)
  4. How close is the putty model to real uncured silicone — what would a calibration experiment look like?

Color model

Linear RGB averaging is physically wrong for pigment — blue + yellow should make green, not gray. ColorMill uses Mixbox (Kubelka–Munk pigment mixing, SIGGRAPH Asia 2021) for color blending; in v2 the latent-to-RGB polynomial is ported to WGSL and runs in the ray-march shader.

  • It ships an official GLSL shader, so it runs per-fragment on the GPU — the same path the renderer needs.
  • Its latent space (7 coefficients) is what every particle carries: latents are averaged during rastering and dispersion and converted to RGB only at render time.
  • License is CC BY-NC 4.0 (non-commercial); fine for this research project with attribution. Vendored under web/vendor/mixbox/.
  • See the live pigment demo — Mixbox vs. naive linear, side by side.
