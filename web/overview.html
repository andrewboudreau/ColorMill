Overview

How ColorMill works

ColorMill is a two-roll mill in your browser. A bank of clear silicone putty sits on two counter-rotating rolls, gets dragged through the gap between them, and sheets onto the front roll. Drop pigment on the bank, cut and roll the sheet back in, and watch the colour disperse the way it does on a real mill. Everything, physics and picture, runs on your GPU through WebGPU.

Open the simulator

Needs a WebGPU browser: Chrome or Edge 113+, Safari 26+, Firefox 141+. A CPU fallback exists for everything else.

What happens on the mill

  1. The bank. Putty rests in the valley between the rolls. Gravity and the rolls' surfaces keep pulling it toward the gap.
  2. The nip. The rolls squeeze the putty through a narrow gap. The back roll turns faster than the front, so the gap is a shear zone. This is the only place colours really mix.
  3. The sheet. The front roll is tackier, so the putty leaves the nip stuck to it as a thin sheet and comes round the top to rejoin the bank.
  4. Cut & fold, cut & roll. A mill only moves material front to back. To mix across the width, the operator cuts the sheet on the front roll from the end and folds the triangular flap over the cut toward the middle; or cuts the whole sheet off, rolls it into a log and feeds it back in end-first. ColorMill scripts both moves.

The tech, in six pieces

Material points on the GPU

The putty is tens to hundreds of thousands of particles (about 40k to 450k for the default batch, depending on the quality preset) simulated with the Material Point Method in its MLS-MPM form. Each substep the particles scatter mass and momentum to a background grid, the grid applies gravity and the rolls, and the particles read the result back. Every stage is a WebGPU compute shader. A fixed number of substeps runs per frame, so a run is deterministic whatever the frame rate.

A putty, not a fluid

Uncured silicone holds its shape under gravity, yields under the nip and does not spring back. The material model is an elastic solid with a small yield band and a short relaxation time, so it keeps a bank on top of the rolls, flows through the gap, and forms a continuous sheet instead of a puddle or a lace.

Rolls as boundary conditions

The rolls are analytic cylinders applied to the grid. The front roll is sticky within a thin band, so the sheet follows it. The back roll turns faster and lets material separate from it with Coulomb friction. That asymmetry is why the sheet ends up on the front roll, exactly as on a real mill, and the speed ratio is a slider.

Pigment in Mixbox space

Every particle carries a Mixbox latent, a small vector describing the pigments it holds, plus a pigment load. The base is clear, so a tap drops a chunk of concentrated masterbatch. Mixing is driven by the local shear rate: colours stay crisp in the resting bank and blend in the nip. Because it happens in pigment space, blue and yellow make green, not grey.

The operator's moves

Cut & fold cuts the sheet on the front roll from the end while it comes up, so the cut is a diagonal and the flap a triangle, then folds the flap over the cut onto the sheet beside it, roll side up, ends alternating. Cut & roll takes everything on the mill, winds it into a volume-preserving log standing over the nip, and feeds it down onto the pile the rolls are eating. Nothing is lost or invented either way.

Ray-marched rendering

The particles are packed into 3D textures of density and pigment. A single fullscreen pass marches rays through them, finds the putty's surface, lights it as a glossy silicone with a colour that gets deeper where the pigment load is higher, and composites the brushed-metal rolls and tray analytically so they stay crisp at any grid resolution.

Using it

  • Tap a colour to set a chunk of pigmented putty down on the bank. The strip of dots picks where along the roll it lands, the three dollops pick how big it is, and chunks stack on whatever is already there.
  • Cut & fold (C) cuts the sheet on the front roll from the end and folds the triangular flap over the cut toward the middle, alternating ends. Cut & roll (F) rolls the sheet up and feeds it back in end-first. A few of either and stripes become a blend.
  • The drawer (P) has roll speed, speed ratio, nip gap, dispersion, gravity, batch size and quality preset, plus live stats.
  • Drag to orbit, wheel or pinch to zoom, double-tap for the front view. Space pauses, R resets.
